A payment signed
before it existed

Three people share one shielded Zcash account. Any two of them can spend from it. This page walks the one payment the prototype actually made: how the signature was produced before the transaction had a proof or an anchor, what each signer checked before agreeing, and which parts of the design are still unproven.

01

One account, three holders, any two

There is a single shielded account. No one holds a key that can spend from it on their own, and no complete spending key exists anywhere, not even briefly during setup. Each of the three holds a share, and two shares combined produce one ordinary signature that the network cannot distinguish from a single-signer one.

02

Three candidates, so nobody has to be online

The proposer does not know in advance which two people will approve. So it builds one candidate transaction for each possible pair: Alice&Bob, Alice&Carol, Bob&Carol. All three spend the same coin and describe the same payment, down to the byte. They differ in exactly one thing: the per-pair blinding value that re-randomises the signing key. Whichever pair approves first can be completed; the others are simply discarded.

What is identical across all three

    What differs

    Only alpha, the per-pair randomiser, and therefore rk, the re-randomised verification key that appears in the transaction. Each signer recomputes alpha itself from the proposal and the published nonce commitments, then checks that the rk in the candidate matches. A proposer cannot choose it.

    04

    What a signer checks before agreeing

    A signer is handed transaction bytes by someone else. Approving the wrong bytes is the whole risk, so the signer re-derives every fact from the transaction's own cryptography rather than trusting a description attached to it. The change address, for instance, is computed from the account key by the signer; it is never read from the proposal.

    A rejected candidate emits no share. The refusal happens before any signing material is produced, so there is nothing to leak and nothing to retract. The adversarial run asserts both halves: a refusal and zero share files on disk.

    05

    Finishing without the people who approved

    Signing and finishing are separate jobs. Once two people have approved, the remaining work is proving and broadcasting, and that needs no secret at all. Bob, who did not approve, ran it from a copy of the public material plus his own scan of the chain. The approvers' directories were moved out of reach first, so he could not have read them.

    Honest limit: this is a second process with its own directory on the same machine, reading a replica made by a local copy. There is no relay, no transport authentication, and no second piece of hardware.
